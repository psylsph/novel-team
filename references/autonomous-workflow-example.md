# Autonomous Team Workflow: Stage 2 Character Development

**Real example from a military thriller project**

This file demonstrates how the novel team autonomously resolved character development questions while escalating only critical decisions to the author.

---

## Session Structure

**Team Lead:** Alex (Developmental Editor)
**Support:** Riley (Research), Casey (Continuity), Quinn (Fact-Checker)
**Total Questions:** ~50
**Autonomously Resolved:** ~95%
**Escalated to Author:** Only story-breaking decisions

---

## Phase 1: Research Foundation

**Riley's Research Block:**

Used `/delegate_task` to research:
| Character | Questions Resolved |
|---|---|---|
| PROTAGONIST | Backstory, motivation, flaw, relationship with family, professional reputation |
| SUPPORTING-1 (Ex-Military) | Background, unit, discharge reason, relationship with protagonist |
| SUPPORTING-2 (Hacker/Villain) | Motivation, methods, vulnerabilities, ecosystem |

**Key Constraints Discovered:**
- Intelligence: Legal oversight, bureaucracy, warrants needed—not unilateral action
- Special Forces: Physical limitations, chronic injuries, no official status after discharge
- Cybercriminals: Financial motivation (not ideological), vulnerable to arrest/betrayal

---

## Phase 2: Autonomous Character Decisions

**Alex's Decision-Making Framework:**

For each character, Alex made creative decisions based on:
- Story outline tone and themes
- Research constraints from Riley
- Character's role in narrative arc
- Consistency with established details

### Example: PROTAGONIST

**Questions resolved:**
1. Professional falling out → He was right (visionary but difficult)
2. Exit → Forced exit with "voluntary package" (wound, not stand)
3. Post-career → 3 years, moderately successful (comfortable but not wealthy)
4. Relationship with family → Present but distant (doesn't truly *see* them)
5. Operational reputation → Innovative problem-solver (unconventional solutions)
6. Fatal flaw → Self-righteous certainty (convinced he's right, doesn't hear dissent)
7. Mid-story internal monologue → No hesitation, justifies breaking faith

**Decision Logic:**
- "He was right" → Makes him visionary but difficult, explains failed promotion
- "Forced exit" → Creates resentment, explains willingness to return
- "Present but distant" → Establishes what he doesn't realize he's losing
- "Self-righteous certainty" → Sets up the climax decision and its cost

### Example: EX-MILITARY SUPPORTING CHARACTER

**Questions resolved:**
1. Regiment → Special Forces Regiment (14 years, Staff Sergeant)
2. Why "ex" → Medical discharge after career-ending injury
3. Relationship with protagonist → First time working, mutual respect earned
4. Tactical leadership → Quiet, methodical, laconic (competence not charisma)
5. The wounding → Tactical failure (his mistake), not noble sacrifice

**Decision Logic:**
- Elite unit background → Establishes competence
- Medical discharge → Realistic, not heroic
- Tactical error → Creates dramatic irony (nearly dies from own mistake)

---

## Phase 3: Critical Escalation

**Only story-breaking questions required author input:**

### 1. Protagonist's Marriage Endstate

**Why escalated:** Fundamentally alters emotional conclusion of the story.

**Options presented:**
- **Option A:** Damaged but surviving - rebuild possible, never the same
- **Option B:** Marriage ends - separation/divorce, total cost

**Context:** Partner gives exhausted ultimatum mid-story, stays but damaged, accepts in final scene that they'll never stop pursuing their calling. This decision changes the emotional weight of the ending.

### 2. Supporting Character's Fate (climax)

**Why escalated:** Affects aftermath's emotional weight and thematic resonance.

**Options presented:**
- **Option A:** Permanent disability - visible permanent cost
- **Option B:** Full recovery - price nearly paid but dodged

**Context:** Supporting character critically wounded due to tactical error, barely saved. This decision determines whether the operation's cost is visible and permanent or nearly paid.

## Communication Format

**Consolidated presentation with clear speaker attribution:**

```markdown
## PROTAGONIST

**Questions from Alex (Developmental Editor):**
1. The professional falling out: What specifically...?
2. Their exit: Forced or protest?
[etc]

**Alex's decisions:**
1. Professional falling out: He was right. [Year], he identified...
2. Exit: Forced exit after failed promotion...
[etc]

**Casey (Continuity Editor):**
"Recording these to character file. Additional details resolved:
- Age: [Age]
- Timeline: Set in [Year]
[etc]"
```

**Key principles:**
- Group questions by character/topic
- Show who's asking what
- Consolidate related decisions
- Make attribution explicit

---

## Results

**Autonomous resolution covered:**
- Character motivations, traits, mannerisms
- Career paths, professional expertise
- Relationship dynamics (non-core)
- Technical details within realism
- Secondary character choices
- Practical details (ages, names, timeline)

**Author escalation preserved control over:**
- Protagonist's arc resolution
- Emotional conclusion
- Thematic cost/outcome
- Major character fates

**Outcome:** Team did hours of research and decision-making autonomously. Author only weighed in on story-breaking questions. Progress maintained while creative control protected.

---

## When to Use This Approach

**Best for:**
- Stage 2: Character Development
- Stage 4: Setting & Worldbuilding
- Stage 6: Scene Planning
- Any stage with many interconnected decisions

**Not for:**
- Stage 1: Initial concept (author's vision)
- Stage 15: Final review (author's voice)
- Any decision that changes story's core

**Key indicator:** If the question requires choosing between fundamentally different story outcomes, escalate. If it's about *how* to implement an established direction, resolve autonomously.
