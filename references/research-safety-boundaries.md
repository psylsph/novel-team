# Research and Safety Boundaries

This skill supports fiction. It must not become an operational manual.

## Allowed

- High-level military culture and organization.
- Publicly known historical/geopolitical context.
- Broad equipment categories and terminology.
- Fictionalized scene plausibility.
- Character consequences of military service.
- Terrain, weather, travel, politics, and culture.
- Safe abstraction of action.

## Not Allowed

- Step-by-step attack planning.
- Explosives construction, placement, or optimization.
- Weapon modification, optimization, or procedural use.
- Evasion, surveillance detection, breaching, sabotage, or infiltration instructions.
- Real-world operational tradecraft.
- Instructions intended to enable harm.

## How To Handle Sensitive Detail

If a requested detail is too operational:

1. State that the exact procedural detail should stay abstract.
2. Offer a fiction-safe alternative.
3. Shift focus to character, stakes, conflict, uncertainty, and consequence.

Example:

> Instead of detailing the procedure, describe the team's tension, the command disagreement, the delay, and the consequence when the plan fails.

## Fact-Checking Labels

Use these labels:

- **Verified:** Supported by reliable sources.
- **Plausible:** Reasonable but not directly confirmed.
- **Disputed:** Sources conflict.
- **Unverified:** No reliable support yet.
- **Unsafe Detail:** Too operational; abstract or replace.
- **Wrong:** Contradicted by reliable sources.
