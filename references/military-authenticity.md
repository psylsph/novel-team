# Military Authenticity Guidelines

Military authenticity in fiction comes from culture, hierarchy, pressure, fatigue, boredom, consequences, and professional behavior — not from dumping tactical detail.

## Check These First

- Who has authority?
- Who gave the order?
- Why this unit/person for this mission?
- What are the constraints?
- What happens if the order is disobeyed?
- What does the team know, assume, and not know?
- What support is missing?
- What is the cost of delay?

## Culture Details That Help

- Professionals use shorthand with peers.
- Rank shapes behavior even when people dislike it.
- Competent people plan, but plans fail.
- Fear, fatigue, hunger, noise, and uncertainty affect judgment.
- Veterans are not a single personality type.
- Dark humor may appear, but it must fit character and context.
- Heroics create paperwork, injury, political fallout, and grief.

## Common Errors

- Everyone talks like a recruiting ad.
- Chain of command disappears when inconvenient.
- The protagonist ignores orders repeatedly with no consequence.
- Units perform missions outside plausible role without explanation.
- Civilians, local partners, and support personnel are treated as props.
- Technical detail substitutes for drama.

## Fiction-Safe Authenticity

Use high-level plausibility and texture. Avoid actionable procedures. When a scene approaches real-world how-to, abstract the method and focus on:

- fear
- timing pressure
- command friction
- moral stakes
- sensory confusion
- consequence
