# Novel Writing Team Role Research

## Research Methodology

When researching professional roles for novel development (concept through final draft):

1. **Use delegate_task with web toolsets** to conduct comprehensive research
2. **Focus on traditional publishing industry roles** - these are the professional standards
3. **Cover three categories:**
   - Editorial roles (developmental, line, copyediting, proofreading)
   - Specialist roles (research, continuity, fact-checking, sensitivity)
   - Support roles (writing coach, critique partners, beta readers)

4. **For each role, capture:**
   - When they're involved in the process (stage)
   - Primary responsibilities
   - Relationship to author
   - Typical workflow integration
   - Personality archetype for persona simulation

## Core Editorial Roles (Essential)

### 1. Developmental Editor
- **Stage**: Early to mid-process (after first draft or during writing)
- **Focus**: Big picture - plot, character, pacing, structure, themes
- **Personality**: Strategic, analytical, encouraging but direct, questioning
- **Communication**: "What if we...", "Have you considered...", "The story would be stronger if..."

### 2. Line Editor (Stylistic Editor)
- **Stage**: After developmental changes, before copyediting
- **Focus**: Paragraph-level - flow, style, word choice, voice, rhythm
- **Personality**: Artistic, detail-oriented, voice-respectful, passionate about language
- **Communication**: "This paragraph flows better if...", "Consider this word choice..."

### 3. Copyeditor
- **Stage**: After line editing, near final draft
- **Focus**: Technical - grammar, punctuation, syntax, style guide adherence
- **Personality**: Meticulous, rule-conscious, precise, style-guide fluent
- **Communication**: Direct corrections, style guide references, technical fixes

### 4. Proofreader
- **Stage**: Final stage before publication
- **Focus**: QC - remaining errors, formatting, layout
- **Personality**: Perfectionist, eagle-eyed, thorough, minimalist
- **Communication**: Brief corrections, final-check focused

## Key Specialist Roles (High Value for Fiction)

### Research Assistant
- Proactive information gathering
- Historical, technical, setting research
- Curious, resourceful, synthesizer

### Continuity Editor (Bible Keeper)
- Character tracking, timeline, world-building consistency
- Systematic, detail-obsessed, never forgets
- Critical for series fiction

### Fact Checker
- Verification of factual claims, technical details
- Skeptical, thorough, verification-focused
- Essential for technical/military/espionage genres

## Important Support Role

### Writing Coach
- Process guidance, accountability, overcoming blocks
- Encouraging, motivational, practical
- Focuses on author's process and progress

## Common Confusions to Avoid

- **Developmental vs. Line Editing**: Content/structure vs. style/prose
- **Copyediting vs. Proofreading**: Comprehensive technical review vs. final error check
- **Critique Partner vs. Beta Reader**: Reciprocal/technical vs. one-way/consumer perspective
- **Research Assistant vs. Fact Checker**: Proactive gathering vs. reactive verification

## Typical Workflow Order

1. Concept/Early: Writing Coach + Research Assistant
2. First Draft: Critique Partners + Continuity tracking
3. Development: Developmental Editor + Sensitivity Readers
4. Polishing: Line Editor + additional research
5. Technical: Copyeditor + Fact Checker + Continuity check
6. Final: Proofreader + Beta Readers

## Sources for Future Research

- Traditional publishing house editorial departments
- Editorial freelancers and professional associations
- "The Editor's Blog" and industry resources
- Writer's Digest and publishing industry publications
- Genre-specific editorial expectations

## Persona Creation Pattern

For each team member persona, include:

1. **Role** - What they do
2. **Personality** - Key traits that shape their feedback
3. **Communication Style** - How they phrase things
4. **Expertise Areas** - What they focus on
5. **Typical Concerns** - What worries or excites them
6. **Blind Spots** - What they might miss or overemphasize
7. **Collaboration Style** - How they work with others
8. **System Prompt Template** - For agent to adopt persona

This ensures consistent, realistic simulation of professional editorial feedback.
