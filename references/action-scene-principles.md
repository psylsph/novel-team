# Action Scene Principles

Action scenes are not demonstrations. They are scenes of decision, reversal, consequence, and cost.

## Required Elements

1. **Objective:** What does the POV character need right now?
2. **Opposition:** What does the other side want?
3. **Geography:** Where is everyone in relation to danger, cover, exits, civilians, and each other?
4. **Constraint:** What prevents the obvious solution?
5. **Reversal:** What goes wrong or changes the plan?
6. **Cost:** Who is hurt, exposed, delayed, compromised, or morally damaged?
7. **Aftermath:** How does the mission state change?

## Action Geography

The reader must understand:

- entry point
- exits
- obstacles
- major threats
- civilian presence
- where the POV character starts
- where the POV character ends
- why movement matters

Do not over-map. Use only the details that orient danger.

## Escalation Pattern

A useful pattern:

```text
Plan → first friction → adaptation → reversal → cost → changed objective → exit hook
```

## Common Failures

- Everyone fires, nothing changes.
- The protagonist survives by author preference.
- Injuries vanish after the scene.
- The enemy behaves stupidly so the hero can look smart.
- The scene explains tactics instead of dramatizing pressure.
- The writer knows the geography but the reader does not.

## Safety Boundary

Keep action fictional, character-centered, and non-operational. Do not provide real-world procedural instruction for attacks, evasion, explosives, breaching, sabotage, or weapons optimization.
