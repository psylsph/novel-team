# Military Thriller Production Stages

Use these stages in order. Every deliverable must pass the review gate before presentation to the author.

## Stage 0 — Seed Capture

Capture the raw idea.

Outputs:
- `concepts/seed.md`
- Initial `AGENTS.md`
- Initial `progress.md`
- Initial `rolling-summary.md`

Checklist:
- premise spark captured
- target subgenre noted
- tone noted
- likely protagonist noted
- likely setting noted
- obvious research risks noted

## Stage 1 — Concept Development

Turn the seed into 3–5 premise options.

Each premise includes:
- protagonist
- goal
- obstacle
- antagonist/force
- setting (including time of year — season affects roads, daylight, grid strain, exposure risk, and atmospheric texture; establish early, do not leave as an open question until Stage 4)
- stakes
- moral dilemma
- commercial hook

Review: Alex, Morgan, Vale.

## Stage 2 — Character and Cast Development

Create major profiles:
- protagonist
- antagonist
- key allies
- commanders/handlers
- local contacts
- betrayal candidates
- civilian pressure characters

Use `templates/character-profile.md`.

Review: Alex, Morgan, Hayes.

## Stage 3 — Threat and Mission Design

Build the engine of the thriller.

Outputs:
- `mission/mission-brief.md`
- `mission/threat-profile.md`
- `mission/faction-map.md`

Review: Alex, Hayes, Vale, Quinn.

## Stage 4 — Research Deep Dive

Research geography, politics, military context, culture, weather, terrain, agencies, and technical claims at fiction-safe level.

Outputs:
- `research/research-brief.md`
- `research/open-questions.md`
- `research/source-list.md`
- `research/safety-labels.md`

Note: Stage 4 research frequently discovers factual errors in Stages 1-3 deliverables (institutional names, geographic details, rank structures). When this occurs, apply the correction protocol in `references/stage-4-5-research-world-workflow.md` under "When Research Discovers Earlier-Stage Errors."

Review: Riley, Quinn, Vale.

## Stage 5 — World and Operational Environment

Define the operating theater.

Outputs:
- `world-outline.md`
- setting profiles
- faction map updates
- operational constraints

Review: Riley, Casey, Vale, Hayes.

## Stage 6 — Story Structure

Create high-level act structure:
- opening hook
- inciting incident
- mission acceptance/forcing event
- first reversal
- midpoint disaster/reveal
- loss/betrayal/team fracture
- false victory
- final mission
- climax
- consequence/resolution

Review: Alex, Morgan, Vale.

## Stage 7 — Chapter Outline

Each chapter includes:
- POV
- location/time
- immediate objective
- conflict
- new information
- reversal/cost
- character beat
- hook
- continuity notes

Review: Alex, Casey, Morgan, Vale.

## Stage 8 — Scene-by-Scene Breakdown

Each scene includes:
- objective
- obstacle
- pressure
- turn/reversal
- exit hook
- continuity impact
- research need

Action scenes also require:
- starting positions
- opposing objectives
- geography
- what goes wrong
- cost

Review: Alex, Casey, Cross, Hayes when applicable.

## Stage 9 — Planning Approval

Present full plan to author only after review fixes.

Gate:
- Author explicitly says ready to write.

## Stage 10 — First Draft

Write one chapter at a time using the chapter drafting cycle in `SKILL.md`.

Review: Alex, Jordan, Casey; add Hayes/Cross/Vale/Quinn as needed.

## Stage 11 — Cross-Chapter Continuity / Mission Logic Pass

Every 5–10 chapters check:
- timeline
- injuries/fatigue
- travel
- who knows what
- mission objective drift
- enemy reaction
- political consequences
- open clues

Review: Casey, Alex, Hayes, Vale.

## Stage 12 — Act Break Full-Stack Review

At each act break assess:
- pacing
- escalation
- stakes
- protagonist agency
- antagonist pressure
- team dynamics
- plausibility
- emotional cost

Review: Alex, Morgan, Casey, Hayes, Vale, Jordan.

## Stage 13 — Developmental Edit

Full manuscript structural review.

Review: Alex primary; Morgan, Casey, Hayes, Vale support.

## Stage 14 — Line Editing

Prose, rhythm, dialogue, action clarity, chapter hooks.

Review: Jordan primary; Cross for action-heavy sections.

## Stage 15 — Copyediting and Fact Check

Grammar, consistency, terminology, ranks, places, factual claims, safety boundary.

Review: Quinn, Casey, Jordan.

## Stage 16 — Proofreading

Fresh-eyes final error catch.

Review: Morgan, Jordan.

## Stage 17 — Final Review and Author Sign-Off

Final approval and retrospective.

Outputs:
- final manuscript
- final concerns log
- retrospective notes
