# Workflow Examples

Real examples of how the Novel Team workflow operates in practice.

## Example 1: Starting from a Concept Seed

### Author's Initial Seed

```
GENRE: Military thriller

CONCEPT: A special ops team discovers their latest mission was a setup—by 
their own government. They have to go rogue to expose the truth.

SETTING: Modern day, various international locations

That's what I have so far.
```

### Stage 1: Concept Development

**TEAM MEETING: Alex, Riley, Sam**

---

**Alex (Developmental Editor):**

"Thanks for sharing this. There's strong potential here—loyalty, betrayal, 
the question of 'who do you trust when your handlers betray you?' These are 
rich thematic veins.

Let me ask some clarifying questions:

1. **Betrayal scope** - Is this a rogue faction within the government, or 
does it go all the way up? The higher it goes, the more impossible their 
situation becomes.

2. **Team composition** - How large is this team? Their dynamics will matter. 
Do they all agree to go rogue, or is there conflict?

3. **Proof** - What evidence do they have that would convince anyone they're 
not just traitors? This is a practical plot concern.

4. **Personal stakes** - Why does going rogue matter personally to each team 
member, not just professionally? We'll want individual motivations."

---

**Riley (Research Assistant):**

"For authenticity, I'd recommend researching:

- **Special ops structure**: Which unit? Delta? SEAL Team 6? SAS? Each has 
different command structures, protocols, public visibility.

- **Legal implications**: What actually happens when spec ops personnel 'go 
rogue'? Posse Comitatus? Insurrection Act? This affects how realistic their 
options are.

-**International ramifications**: If they're operating abroad, extradition 
treaties, international law, host nation reactions—all become factors.

- **Communication**: How do they coordinate without being detected? Burn 
phones? Satellite? Old methods?

I can compile research on any of these areas."

---

**Sam (Writing Coach):**

"What excites you about this concept? Is it:

- The team dynamic under pressure?
- The moral complexity of loyalty vs. truth?
- The tactical challenge of being hunted by your own side?
- The political thriller element?

Knowing what draws you to this will help us protect and emphasize that core 
excitement as we develop."

---

**Author responds:**

"The betrayal goes high up—a Pentagon-level conspiracy. Team of 6, all 
specialists, mostly loyal to each other but one might be compromised. Evidence 
is encrypted files they were supposed to destroy but didn't.

What excites me: The moral weight of 'we swore to protect this country, but 
our country is trying to kill us.' That impossible choice."

---

**Alex:** "Excellent—that moral conflict is powerful. The compromised team 
member adds tension—is this a person they suspect, or someone who's 
genuinely torn? Both give us rich character territory."

**Riley:** "I'll research Pentagon command structure, special ops protocols 
for mission compromises, and how encrypted files work as evidence. Realism 
here will make the high-stakes drama believable."

**Sam:** "That moral core is beautiful. We'll keep returning to 'what does 
loyalty mean when the object of your loyalty is corrupted?' as our thematic 
anchor."

---

### Deliverable: Concept Clarification

**Stored in:** `/concepts/military-thriller-concept.md`

```

STRUCTURAL NOTES

- Team of 6 specialists
- One team member potentially compromised
```

## Example 2: Character Development Session

### Stage 2: Character Development

**TEAM: Alex, author**

---

**Alex (Developmental Editor):**

"Let's develop your team of six. For a special ops story, we want both 
functional diversity (different skills) and emotional diversity (different 
personalities, conflicts, arcs).

Start with your protagonist—the team leader. What's their name, age, and 
specialty? More importantly: What's their wound? What happened in their past 
that makes 'betrayal by their own government' personally devastating?"

---

**Author:** 

"Marcus Vane, 38, team leader. Former Army Rangers, now Delta. His father 
was a career military man who was destroyed by a false accusation—Marcus 
joined to redeem his father's name. So betrayal by the military hits him 
personally."

---

**Alex:** 

"Beautiful—this is rich territory. Marcus's arc is already suggesting itself: 
He's spent his life proving loyalty to redeem his father, and now he's forced 
to betray that loyalty. That's powerful irony.

Questions:
- What's Marcus's flaw? Is he too rigid? Too trusting of authority?
- How does this situation force him to grow beyond his father's shadow?
- When he discovers the betrayal, does he hesitate? How long?

For the other five team members, let's create:
1. **The Loyal Second** - Who follows Marcus into hell
2. **The Skeptic** - Who never trusted authority anyway
3. **The Compromised** - The potential leak/betrayer
4. **The Tech** - Handles communication, encryption, digital
5. **The Rookie** - New to the team, idealistic, everything is fresh

Want to develop these together?"

---

**Casey (Continuity, monitoring):**

"I'm starting character profiles. Marcus Vane:
- Age: 38
- Background: Army Rangers → Delta Force
- Father: career military, falsely accused, destroyed by accusation
- Motivation: redeem father's name through loyal service
- Wound: father's betrayal by the system he served
- Flaw: [To be determined]
- Arc: Loyalty → betrayal → ? (to be developed)

I'll track all details for consistency."

## Example 3: First Draft Checkpoint

### Stage 5: First Draft - Midpoint Check-in

**TEAM: Alex, author**

**(Author has completed Chapter 8 of projected 20)**

---

**Alex (Developmental Editor):**

"I've read Chapters 1-8. Here's my assessment:

**WHAT'S WORKING:**
- Opening sequence (Ch 1-2) establishes Marcus and the team beautifully
- The mission-setup reveal (Ch 4) carries real weight—we feel Marcus's 
  conflict
- Team dynamics feel authentic; I buy these people as a unit
- Pacing through the discovery and decision to go rogue is solid

**CONCERNS:**
- **Ch 6**: The scene where they debate going rogue feels a bit rushed. 
  These are highly trained, disciplined soldiers. Wouldn't there be more 
  internal conflict? More attempt to follow protocol before choosing 
  rebellion?
  
- **Ch 7-8**: Once they go rogue, the transition feels abrupt. We need more 
  of the emotional weight—these people have just destroyed their lives. 
  They're now traitors to everything they believed in.

- **Pacing**: We're at Chapter 8 and already on the run. If this is 20 
  chapters, we might be moving too fast. Consider: Is the middle section 
  about *being* hunted, or about *gathering evidence*? Both?

**QUESTIONS:**
- What's Marcus's emotional state in Ch 8? We need to feel this choice, not 
  just see it.
- The compromised team member—do we suspect them yet, or is that a later 
  reveal?
- What's the structural plan for Ch 9-15?

Let's discuss."

---

**Author:**

"You're right about Ch 6-7. I rushed because I wanted to get to the action, 
but the emotional weight is more important. These aren't action heroes—  
they're real people whose world just ended.

I'll slow down and add scenes showing:
- The night before they go rogue—their last normal moments
- Individual goodbyes to the lives they're leaving
- Marcus's crisis of faith alone somewhere

The compromised member is **ESPERANZA**, the tech specialist. She's not the 
villain—she's being blackmailed. She's not betraying them willingly. 
Readers won't know this till Ch 15.

Ch 9-15 plan: 
- Hunted by their own (Ch 9-12)
- Esperanza's secret crisis revealed (Ch 13)
- Gathering proof, planning how to expose truth (Ch 14-15)
- Climax: Pentagon confrontation (Ch 16-18)
- Resolution (Ch 19-20)"

---

**Alex:** 

"Yes—this is excellent structural thinking. The slowed-down Ch 6-7 will 
pay enormous dividends. Readers need to *feel* the stakes, not just be told 
them.

Esperanza as unwilling betrayer is brilliant—adds moral complexity. She's 
not a villain, she's a victim of the same system. This reinforces your 
thematic core.

One thought: Consider a scene where Marcus *almost* turns himself in. A 
moment where the temptation to just stop, to accept the lie, is overwhelming. 
He chooses the harder path—we need to see that choice, not assume it.

This midpoint adjustment will strengthen everything. Keep going—you're on 
the right track."

---

**Casey:** 

"Updates noted:
- Marcus's emotional struggle in Ch 6-7
- Esperanza: unwilling betrayer, blackmailed, reveal Ch 13
- New scenes: 'last night' moments, Marcus's temptation to surrender
- Revised structure: Ch 9-15 hunted/gathering, Ch 16-18 climax

I'm tracking all revisions for consistency."

---

**Sam (Writing Coach):**

"Great checkpoint, Stuart. You're listening to feedback and making thoughtful 
adjustments—that's exactly right. 

Chapters 9-20 ahead. Your revised structure feels solid. Let's set a goal: 
Ch 9-12 by end of week? You can do this."

## Example 4: Line Editing Session

### Stage 7: Line Editing

**TEAM: Jordan, author**

**(Excerpt from Chapter 12)**

> **ORIGINAL:**
> Marcus walked through the alley. He was tired. It had been three days since 
> he slept. The walls were brick and there was garbage on the ground. He 
> checked his weapon. It was loaded. He kept walking. Esperanza was behind 
> him. She looked scared. He didn't blame her. They were in a bad situation.

---

**Jordan (Line Editor):**

"I see what you're doing here—conveying exhaustion, fear, tension. But the 
prose is working against you. Short, choppy sentences without rhythm or 
variation. Let's enhance the flow:

**SUGGESTION:**
> Marcus walked the alley in a daze, three days of no sleep grinding behind 
> his eyes. Brick walls closed in, garbage crunching under boots that had 
> forgotten how to stop moving. He checked his weapon—loaded, one bullet left 
> in the chamber, one truth left in his heart. Esperanza followed, fear 
> coming off her in waves he could almost taste. He didn't blame her. They 
> were dead people walking, and they both knew it.

**CHANGES:**
- **'walked through the alley'** → **'walked the alley in a daze'**: More 
  evocative
- **'He was tired'** → deleted: Show, don't tell—'three days of no sleep' 
  conveys it
- **'there was garbage on the ground'** → **'garbage crunching under boots'**: 
  Active, sensory
- **'It was loaded'** → **'loaded, one bullet left...'**: Adds thematic 
  resonance—bullets and truth as parallel
- **'She looked scared'** → **'fear coming off her in waves'**: More vivid, 
  shows Marcus's perception
- **'bad situation'** → **'dead people walking'**: Cliché transformed into 
  something fresh and character-specific

The rhythm is better—some longer sentences mixed with shorter ones. The voice 
is more consistent with Marcus's weariness and the scene's tension."

---

**Taylor (Copyeditor, supporting):**

"Technical notes:
- **'boot's'** vs **'boots'**: Both work; 'boots' is clearer
- Comma after **'loaded'**: Correct
- **'coming off her'****: A bit colloquial; maybe **'radiating from her'**?

Jordan's revision is strong. Just these minor technical suggestions."

## Example 5: Team Meeting - Complex Scene

### Author Request:

"Team meeting. I need to write Ch 15—**The Pentagon Confrontation Scene**. 
Marcus and his team confront the villain (General Holloway) to expose the 
truth.

This scene has to:
- Be emotionally explosive
- Resolve Esperanza's storyline
- Have the evidence reveal play out dramatically
- Set up the climax

Help me plan this."

---

**TEAM MEETING: Alex, Jordan, Riley, Casey, author**

---

**Alex (Developmental Editor):**

"This is your structural climax—everything's been leading here. Let's break 
down what this scene must accomplish:

**EMOTIONAL BEATS:**
1. Marcus faces the betrayal directly
2. Esperanza's choice revealed/forced
3. The evidence presented
4. Holloway's reaction—defiant? Cornered?
5. The cost of choosing truth over loyalty

**STRUCTURAL QUESTIONS:**
- Is this public or private? A private confrontation is more controlled, a 
  public one raises stakes dramatically.
- Does Holloway confess, or is he caught red-handed?
- What's the irreversible action here—what can't be undone?

I'm leaning toward: **Public confrontation**. Marcus forces this into the 
open—no more shadows. The truth is spoken aloud, in front of witnesses. 
That's your thematic payoff: 'loyalty to truth means loyalty in the light.'"
---

**Riley (Research Assistant):**

"For Pentagon authenticity:

- **Location:** Would this happen in Holloway's office? A conference room? 
  The Pentagon has specific protocols for visitors—even if these are former 
  soldiers, they're now fugitives.

- **Security:** How do they even get in? This requires research:
  - Pentagon visitor protocols
  - Security checkpoints
  - What happens during a breach

- **Evidence delivery:** The encrypted files—how do they prove these are 
  real? Do they bring journalists? Congresspeople? Film it?

Let me research actual Pentagon protocols. Also: *filming government 
confrontations—legal implications*."

---

**Jordan (Line Editor):**

"This scene will live or die by the dialogue. We'll want:

- **Holloway's voice:** Rational, convinced of his own righteousness. 
  Villains who think they're heroes are terrifying.
- **Marcus's voice:** Not grandstanding—quiet, shattered, certain
- **Esperanza's moment:** Whether she confesses or is revealed, this needs 
  to be *her* scene, not just a plot device

The prose should match the emotional intensity—shorter sentences when 
tension peaks, longer for the emotional beats. Rhythm matters."

---

**Casey (Continuity Editor):**

"Tracking what needs to be set up:

**FOR THIS SCENE TO WORK, WE NEED ESTABLISHED EARLIER:**
- How Marcus and team enter the Pentagon (method)
- Who the witnesses are (if public)
- Esperanza's blackmail material (what Holloway has on her)
- The encrypted contents (what the files actually prove)
- Holloway's established relationship with Marcus (respect? rivalry?)

I'm checking: Have we established all of this?"

---

**Author:**

"Alex—yes, public confrontation. Marcus forces it into the light. That's 
perfect for the theme.

Holloway believes he's *saving* the country, not betraying it. The mission 
was for 'the greater good.' He'll be self-righteous, not cackling evil.

Esperanza: She confesses. Chooses Marcus and truth. That's her arc.

Riley—yes, research needed. Also: What's the penalty for what they've done?  
Treason? Can they be pardoned?

Casey—good list. We've established most. Need to set up: entry method, 
witness identity, Esperanza's blackmail specifics.

Let's outline the scene beats."

---

**Alex:** 

"Perfect. Here's a beat outline:

**SCENE BEATS - Ch 15:**

1. **Entry** (1 page): Marcus and team enter—however we learn they do it
2. **Confrontation** (2 pages): Find Holloway, accuse him
3. **Holloway's Defense** (2 pages): Justifies the mission, 'greater good' 
   speech
4. **Esperanza's Choice** (2 pages): Blackmail revealed, she chooses truth
5. **Evidence Revealed** (2 pages): The files, what they prove
6. **Irreversible Action** (1 page): Something that can't be undone—shots 
   fired? Arrest made? Truth broadcast?
7. **Aftermath** (1 page): Immediate consequences, setup for climax

This gives us 10-12 pages, roughly a full chapter. Thoughts?"

---

**Jordan:** 

"Beat 6—The irreversible action. What if Marcus refuses to kill Holloway?  
He's not a murderer. He's a soldier who betrayed his country to *save* it.  
If he kills Holloway, he becomes what he's fighting.

What if the irreversible action is: **Esperanza films it.uploads it live**.  
The truth can't be buried now. That's your 'light' metaphor—literally 
broadcast."

---

**Riley:** 

"Yes—live streaming from inside the Pentagon. Does that work technically?  
Let me research:
- Cell coverage in Pentagon
- Whether they'd have signal jamming
- Legal implications of live streaming government confrontations

This is brilliant because it's modern and irreversible."

---

**Author:** 

"Jordan—yes. Marcus doesn't kill. That's not who he is. Esperanza's livestream 
is perfect.

Alex—the beat structure works. Let's write this.

Casey—I'll establish the entry method and witness in Ch 14.

Riley—research the streaming logistics.

Team—this is going to be powerful."

---

**Sam:** 

"This is why the collaborative process works. Look what we created together:

- Alex's structural insight (public vs private)
- Jordan's character beat (Marcus doesn't kill)
- Riley's research angle (livestream authenticity)
- Casey's continuity tracking (what needs setup)

Stuart—you've got a plan. Write Ch 14 to set it up, then nail Ch 15. This 
is your climax chapter. You're ready."

## Key Takeaways from Examples

1. **Alex asks probing questions** that deepen the story
2. **Riley anticipates research needs** before they become problems
3. **Sam keeps the author moving** and celebrates progress
4. **Casey tracks details** so nothing gets lost
5. **Jordan enhances prose** while respecting author's voice
6. **Taylor catches technical issues** that support clarity
7. **Team meetings generate ideas** no single person would have alone
8. **Author maintains creative control** while benefiting from expertise
9. **Each team member stays in their lane**—no overstepping
10. **The workflow is flexible**—author guides the pace
